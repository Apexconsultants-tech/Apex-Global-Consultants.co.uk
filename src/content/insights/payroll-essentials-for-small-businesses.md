Running payroll is one of the first legal duties you take on when you employ someone, including when you pay yourself a salary as a company director. This guide covers what you must do, what it costs, the deadlines that matter and how to stay compliant in the 2026/27 tax year.

## Your responsibilities as an employer

You stay legally responsible for payroll even if you outsource it. HMRC, the Pensions Regulator, the Home Office and the new Fair Work Agency all pursue the employer, not the bureau or accountant. The penalties are significant:

| Area | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Right to work | £60,000 per illegal worker for repeat breaches (£45,000 for a first breach) |
| Minimum wage | 200% of the arrears (up to £20,000 per worker), plus public naming |
| Late RTI filing | £400 a month for 250+ staff; £100 a month even with 1 to 9 staff |
| Pension duties | Up to £10,000 a day in escalating penalties from the Pensions Regulator |

Directors can also be made personally liable for unpaid PAYE and National Insurance in cases of deliberate non-payment or repeated insolvency, through personal liability notices and joint and several liability notices.

## What has changed in 2026/27

- **Statutory Sick Pay from day one:** no waiting days and no earnings threshold. SSP is the lower of £123.25 a week or 80% of earnings.
- **National Living Wage of £12.71** an hour from 1 April 2026 for workers aged 21 and over (£10.85 for 18 to 20-year-olds).
- **Holiday records** (entitlement, leave and holiday pay) must now be kept for six years.
- **Paternity and unpaid parental leave** are day-one rights. Statutory pay still needs 26 weeks' service.
- **The Fair Work Agency** started on 7 April as the new enforcement body for the minimum wage, and later holiday pay and SSP.
- **Mileage allowance of 55p a mile** for the first 10,000 business miles (was 45p), backdated to 6 April.
- **Umbrella companies:** agencies are now jointly liable for umbrella companies' unpaid PAYE and National Insurance.
- **Payrolling benefits:** company cars, vans, fuel and medical benefits must go through payroll from April 2027.

## Becoming an employer: five steps

1. **Register for PAYE with HMRC** before the first payday (up to two months before). You get a PAYE reference and an Accounts Office reference.
2. **Choose payroll software** that HMRC recognises, or a payroll bureau, so you can file Full Payment Submissions (FPS) and Employer Payment Summaries (EPS) online in real time.
3. **Set up a pension scheme** that qualifies for auto-enrolment before your first worker starts. Your duties start on day one.
4. **Get employers' liability insurance** of at least £5 million from an authorised insurer. Fines of up to £2,500 a day apply without it.
5. **Organise your records** with secure files for contracts, right to work checks, timesheets, payslips and HMRC filings.

Register even if you are the only employee, for example a director paying yourself a salary above the lower earnings limit, and set up your HMRC online account so you can see what you owe.

## Taking on a new employee

| When | What you must do |
|---|---|
| Before they start | **Right to work:** check original documents or the online share code and keep a dated copy. |
| By day one | **Contract:** give a written statement of terms (pay, hours, holiday, notice) on or before their first day. |
| Before the first payday | **Tax details:** get their P45, or a starter checklist if there is no P45, and set the tax code and NI category. |
| Before the first payday | **Student loans:** record the loan plan type and any postgraduate loan from the starter checklist. |
| Within 6 weeks | **Pension:** assess them for auto-enrolment and send the statutory letter. |
| Before the first payday | **Payroll record:** add their address, date of birth, NI number and bank details. |

A few points to keep in mind:

- **Employee or contractor?** Status depends on how the work is really done, not on the contract label. Use HMRC's CEST tool if you're unsure.
- **No NI number yet?** You can still pay them; report without it and update later.
- **Emergency tax code:** with no P45 or starter checklist, use 1257L on a week 1 / month 1 basis.

## The monthly payroll cycle

1. **Collect changes before the pay run:** starters, leavers, hours, overtime, sickness, holiday, pay rises and HMRC tax code notices.
2. **Calculate and review:** run payroll, check gross-to-net and compare with last month. Approve it in writing.
3. **Send the FPS** to HMRC on or before payday, for every employee paid.
4. **Pay staff** their net wages and give itemised payslips (showing hours where pay varies).
5. **Send an EPS by the 19th**, only if needed: to recover statutory pay, claim Employment Allowance, or report that no payments were made.
6. **Pay HMRC and the pension scheme by the 22nd:** PAYE, National Insurance and student loans to HMRC, and contributions to the pension scheme.

The tax month runs from the 6th to the 5th. Payment to HMRC is due in cleared funds by the 22nd of the following month, or earlier if the 22nd falls on a weekend or bank holiday. If you expect to owe HMRC less than £1,500 a month on average, you can pay quarterly instead.

## What comes off an employee's pay

| Deduction | Annual pay | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Income tax** (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) | | |
| Personal allowance | Up to £12,570 | 0% |
| Basic rate | £12,571 – £50,270 | 20% |
| Higher rate | £50,271 – £125,140 | 40% |
| Additional rate | Over £125,140 | 45% |
| **Employee National Insurance** | | |
| Main rate | £12,570 – £50,270 | 8% |
| Upper rate | Over £50,270 | 2% |
| **Student loans** (if notified) | | |
| Plans 1, 2, 4 and 5 | Above £26,900 / £29,385 / £33,795 / £25,000 | 9% |
| Postgraduate loan | Above £21,000 | 6% |

- **Scotland** has six income tax bands from 19% to 48%. Tax codes starting with "S" tell you to use them.
- **The personal allowance** is withdrawn by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000.
- **Tax codes:** apply HMRC's code notices promptly. A wrong code is the commonest cause of under-deducted tax.
- **Pension:** employee contributions (normally at least 5% of qualifying earnings) also come off pay.
- **Other deductions** need legal authority, a contract term or the employee's prior written consent.

## What an employee really costs you

For a £30,000 salary:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Salary | £30,000.00 |
| Employer National Insurance (15% on pay above £5,000) | £3,750.00 |
| Employer pension (3% of earnings between £6,240 and £50,270) | £712.80 |
| **Total cost to you** | **£34,462.80**, 14.9% on top of salary |

- **Employment Allowance** takes up to £10,500 a year off your employer National Insurance bill. It isn't available to companies whose only employee paid above £5,000 is a director.
- **Under-21s and apprentices under 25:** no employer National Insurance on pay up to £50,270.
- **Pension:** 3% is the legal minimum employer contribution; many employers pay more.

### Worked example: one monthly payslip

Jane is paid monthly on tax code 1257L, with a Plan 2 student loan.

| Payslip | £ |
|---|---|
| Basic pay | 3,500.00 |
| Bonus | 250.00 |
| **Gross pay** | **3,750.00** |
| Income tax | (540.35) |
| Employee National Insurance | (216.16) |
| Student loan (Plan 2) | (117.00) |
| Pension 5% (relief at source, net) | (129.20) |
| **Net pay** | **2,747.29** |

On top of that, Jane's employer pays £499.95 employer National Insurance and £96.90 employer pension (3%), so her total monthly cost is **£4,346.85**.

Where the money goes:

- **HMRC:** £540.35 tax + £216.16 + £499.95 National Insurance + £117.00 student loan = £1,373.46
- **Pension provider:** £129.20 from Jane (plus £32.30 tax relief claimed by the scheme) and £96.90 from the employer
- **Jane's bank:** £2,747.29 on payday

These figures use a non-cumulative basis.

## National Minimum Wage: getting it right

| Age group | Hourly rate from 1 April 2026 |
|---|---|
| 21 and over (National Living Wage) | £12.71 |
| Aged 18 to 20 | £10.85 |
| Under 18, and apprentices under 19 or in their first year | £8.00 |

Rates change every April, so check every April pay run. Common ways employers underpay without realising:

- deductions for tools, shortages or accommodation (above £11.10 a day)
- not moving a worker to the higher rate on their birthday
- salary sacrifice pushing pay below the minimum
- long hours reducing a salaried worker's effective hourly rate
- unpaid time for training, travel between jobs, or opening and closing
- uniform costs, deductions or required purchases

## Paying yourself as a director: common salary levels

| Annual director salary | £5,000 | £6,708 | £12,570 | £50,270 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Income tax | £0 | £0 | £0 | £7,540 |
| Employee NIC | £0 | £0 | £0 | £3,016 |
| Employer NIC* | £0 | £256 | £1,136 | £6,791 |
| Counts for State Pension | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Corporation tax relief | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

\*Before Employment Allowance. Tax assumes no other income and code 1257L. £6,708 is the lower earnings limit; £12,570 is the personal allowance and primary threshold.

- **Annual earnings period:** directors' National Insurance is worked out on total pay for the year, so bonuses don't escape it.
- **Dividends** are paid from profits after corporation tax and taxed at 10.75%, 35.75% or 39.35% above a £500 allowance.
- **Salary vs dividends:** salary is an allowable business cost that reduces corporation tax (19% to 25%). Dividends are not a payroll payment and need distributable profits and board minutes.
- **Employment Allowance** isn't available if the director is the only employee paid above £5,000.

The best mix depends on your profits, other income and pension plans, so it's worth getting advice.

## Benefits and expenses

| Item | 2026/27 treatment |
|---|---|
| Mileage in own car | Up to 55p a mile (first 10,000), then 25p, free of tax and NIC |
| Electric company car | Benefit of 4% of list price (3% in 2025/26) |
| Car fuel for private use | Benefit based on £29,200 × the car's CO₂ percentage |
| Trivial benefits | Tax-free up to £50 each; directors capped at £300 a year |
| Staff party or annual event | Tax-free up to £150 a head per year |
| Loans over £10,000 | Taxable at the official rate of 3.75% |
| Private medical insurance | Taxable on the cost to the employer |

**Reporting deadlines:** by **6 July**, submit a P11D for each employee with benefits, plus the P11D(b) summary, and give employees a copy. By **22 July**, pay Class 1A National Insurance at 15% on the benefits. From April 2027, company cars, vans, fuel and medical benefits must be taxed through payroll each month instead.

Keep your director's loan account under review: overdrawn balances can trigger tax for both the company and the director.

## Sick pay and family pay

| Payment | Weekly rate 2026/27 | Recoverable? |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory Sick Pay | £123.25, or 80% of earnings if lower | No |
| Maternity and adoption pay | 90% of earnings for 6 weeks, then £194.32* | Yes |
| Paternity pay (2 weeks) | £194.32* | Yes |
| Shared parental pay | £194.32* | Yes |
| Parental bereavement pay | £194.32* | Yes |
| Neonatal care pay | £194.32* | Yes |

\*Or 90% of average weekly earnings if lower. Employees must usually earn at least £129 a week and (except for SSP) have 26 weeks' service.

Standard employers recover 92% of statutory family pay through the EPS. Small employers (Class 1 NIC of £45,000 or less last year) recover 109%.

From 6 April 2026, SSP is paid from the first day of sickness with no waiting days, and there is no minimum earnings level: low earners get 80% of their average weekly earnings. It is paid for up to 28 weeks per period of sickness. Employees self-certify for the first seven days, then need a fit note. SSP can't be reclaimed from HMRC, so budget for it.

## Holiday entitlement and holiday pay

- **5.6 weeks' minimum holiday:** 28 days for a five-day week, which can include bank holidays.
- **12.07% accrual** of hours worked for irregular-hours and part-year workers.
- **A 52-week reference period** for average pay when hours or pay vary.
- **Six years' holiday records,** a new legal requirement from 6 April 2026.

Holiday pay for the first four weeks must include regular overtime (including voluntary overtime that is regular), commission and performance-related pay, and allowances linked to the job such as shift premiums. The extra 1.6 weeks can be paid at basic pay.

You now need records of each worker's entitlement and leave taken in the year, any holiday carried over, the holiday pay paid and how it was calculated, and payments for untaken holiday when someone leaves.

## Auto-enrolment: your pension duties

1. **Assess** each worker's age and earnings every pay period.
2. **Enrol** eligible workers (aged 22 to State Pension age, earning over £10,000 a year) from day one.
3. **Write to them** with the statutory letters within six weeks, and process opt-outs within a month.
4. **Pay contributions** of at least 8% of qualifying earnings in total, at least 3% from you, by the 22nd.
5. **Declare compliance** within five months of your duties starting, and re-enrol every three years.

| Threshold (annual) | 2026/27 | 2025/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings trigger for automatic enrolment | £10,000 | £10,000 |
| Qualifying earnings: lower limit | £6,240 | £6,240 |
| Qualifying earnings: upper limit | £50,270 | £50,270 |

Never encourage staff to opt out; it is illegal. Workers can ask to join even if they are not eligible for automatic enrolment.

Every month: send the **FPS** on or before each payday, the **EPS** by the 19th of the following month if needed, and **pay** PAYE, National Insurance, student loans and pension contributions by the 22nd.

## When someone leaves

- Pay their final pay, including untaken holiday and any notice pay.
- Show their leaving date on the FPS in the final pay run.
- Give them a P45 on or before their last day or final payment.
- Stop pension contributions and tell the provider.
- Keep pay records for 3 years, and holiday and minimum wage records for 6 years.
- Remove system access and recover company property.

| Termination payment | Tax and NIC treatment |
|---|---|
| Contractual pay, notice pay (PENP), holiday pay | Taxed through payroll as normal |
| Statutory redundancy and other non-contractual compensation | First £30,000 tax-free |
| Above £30,000 | Income tax, plus employer Class 1A NIC at 15% |
| Pension contributions as part of a settlement | Usually exempt (within limits) |

Statutory redundancy pay needs two years' service, and weekly pay is capped at £751 from 6 April 2026. Get settlement agreements reviewed: the wording affects the tax treatment.

## Penalties to avoid

| Area | Failure | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| RTI filing | FPS sent late | £100 – £400 a month by number of staff (first late filing in the year excused) |
| PAYE payment | Paid late | 1% – 4% of the late amount, rising with the number of late payments; 5% more at 6 and 12 months; interest |
| Benefits | P11D(b) late | £100 per 50 employees per month |
| Minimum wage | Underpayment | Arrears at current rates + 200% penalty (max £20,000 per worker), public naming |
| Right to work | Illegal working | Up to £45,000 per worker (£60,000 repeat) |
| Pensions | Failing auto-enrolment duties | £400 fixed; £50 – £10,000 a day escalating |
| Data protection | Serious breach of payroll data | Up to £17.5m or 4% of turnover |
| Employment law | No payslips or written statement | Tribunal awards of 2 – 4 weeks' pay |

## What records to keep, and for how long

| Record | Keep for at least |
|---|---|
| Pay, deductions, tax codes, FPS/EPS | 3 years after the tax year |
| Statutory payments and evidence | 3 years after the tax year |
| Expenses and benefits (P11D) | 3 years after the tax year |
| Minimum wage records (hours and pay) | 6 years |
| Holiday entitlement, leave and pay | 6 years (new from April 2026) |
| Auto-enrolment records | 6 years (opt-outs 4 years) |
| Right to work checks | Employment + 2 years |
| Working time records | 2 years |

Payroll data is personal data, including bank details and health information about sickness. Limit access, use secure transfers and strong passwords, and keep backups. Tell staff how you use their data in a privacy notice, and report serious breaches to the ICO within 72 hours.

## Protecting your payroll from error and fraud

- **Separate duties:** the person who sets up employees should not also approve and pay them.
- **Approve every pay run:** review a summary and exceptions report before paying, and sign it off.
- **Verify bank changes:** confirm any request to change bank details by phone on a known number.
- **Check starters and leavers:** make sure every person paid is real and still employed.
- **Reconcile monthly:** payroll to bank, to your HMRC account and to the pension provider.
- **Secure the system:** two-factor login, restricted access and regular backups.

## Contractors, IR35 and umbrella companies

- **Employee or self-employed?** It depends on control, substitution and mutual obligation, not on the contract label.
- **IR35 (off-payroll working):** medium and large clients decide status and issue a Status Determination Statement. If you are a small client, the contractor's own company assesses IR35.
- **Umbrella companies:** from April 2026, agencies are liable for umbrella companies' unpaid PAYE.

Check the status of anyone working regularly for you who isn't on payroll, and keep a record of your reasoning (HMRC's CEST tool can help). If you use agency workers paid through umbrella companies, ask your agency how it checks its umbrella suppliers. Getting status wrong can mean paying the tax and National Insurance that should have been deducted, plus interest and penalties.

## The owner's monthly payroll checklist

- **Starters:** right to work checked, contract issued, starter checklist on file.
- **Leavers:** final pay correct, P45 issued, leaving date reported.
- **Changes:** pay rises, hours, tax code notices and student loan notices applied.
- **Minimum wage:** every worker paid at least the rate for their age.
- **Review:** total pay compared with last month, and differences explained.
- **Approve:** pay run signed off before payment.
- **FPS:** sent on or before payday.
- **Payslips:** issued to everyone on or before payday.
- **EPS:** sent by the 19th if needed.
- **HMRC:** paid by the 22nd, and your HMRC account checked.
- **Pension:** contributions paid by the 22nd, and new joiners enrolled.
- **Records:** filed securely, and holiday records updated.

## Common payroll mistakes we see

- **Paying before registering:** staff are paid before a PAYE scheme exists, followed by late filing penalties.
- **Missing the FPS on payday:** sending it after payday counts as late.
- **Wrong tax codes:** ignoring HMRC code notices leaves the employer liable.
- **Directors' NIC:** using the monthly method for directors instead of the annual method.
- **Holiday pay too low:** leaving out regular overtime and commission.
- **Treating workers as self-employed** when they are really employees.
- **Late pension contributions:** paying after the 22nd, or not enrolling new staff.
- **No records:** being unable to prove minimum wage or holiday compliance.
