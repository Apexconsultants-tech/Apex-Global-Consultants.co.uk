A partnership is two or more people, or companies, running a business together and sharing the profits. This guide explains how partnerships and limited liability partnerships (LLPs) are set up, taxed and reported for the 2026/27 tax year.

## What is a partnership?

In an ordinary partnership the business isn't a separate legal person: the partners are the business.

- **Two or more partners:** individuals, companies or both.
- **Profits shared** in the ratio you agree, not necessarily equally.
- **Each partner is taxed** on their own share, through their own tax return.
- **Shared liability:** in a general partnership, each partner can be liable for all of the business's debts.

The partnership itself doesn't pay Income Tax. It reports its profit to HMRC on a partnership return, and each partner pays Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance on their share. The partnership can still register for VAT and PAYE, and pays those in its own name.

A Scottish partnership is a separate legal person, but the partners are still taxed on their shares.

## Partnership, LLP or limited company?

| | General partnership | LLP | Limited company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separate legal person? | No (yes in Scotland) | Yes | Yes |
| Owners' liability | Unlimited and shared | Limited to what you put in | Limited |
| Register with | HMRC only | Companies House and HMRC | Companies House and HMRC |
| Public accounts? | No | Yes, filed at Companies House | Yes |
| How profits are taxed | Income Tax and Class 4 NIC on each share | Same as a partnership | Corporation Tax (19% to 25%), then tax on salary or dividends |
| Admin and cost | Lowest | Medium | Highest |
| Privacy | High | Lower (public register) | Lower (public register) |

### Which structure suits you?

Two questions help narrow it down:

1. **Do you want to protect your personal assets from the business's debts?** For example, if the business takes on big contracts, borrows money, employs staff or carries real risk. If not, a **general partnership** is the simplest and cheapest to run, with no public accounts and profits taxed once, on each partner.
2. **Will each owner's profit regularly be over about £50,000, with some left in the business?** If not, an **LLP** protects members' personal assets while still being taxed like a partnership. If so, a **limited company** may suit you better: Corporation Tax on profits kept in the business can be cheaper for higher earners, although taking money out has its own taxes and there is more admin.

The right answer depends on your profits, risk and plans. Get advice before incorporating an existing partnership.

## Your partnership agreement

There's no legal requirement for a written agreement, but without one the Partnership Act 1890 applies. That means equal profit shares, and the partnership ending when a partner leaves or dies.

A good agreement covers:

- **Profit-sharing ratio**, and any partner "salaries" or interest on capital
- **Capital:** how much each partner puts in and how it's repaid
- **Drawings:** how much partners can take out, and when
- **Decision-making:** who decides what, and voting rights
- **Nominated partner:** who deals with HMRC and the tax return
- **Joining and leaving:** new partners, retirement, death and illness
- **Disputes:** how disagreements are resolved
- **Ending the partnership** and how assets are divided

Profit-sharing ratios can't be changed after the end of a tax year to save tax. Agree them in writing at the start, and have a solicitor draft or review the agreement.

## Registering your partnership with HMRC

1. **Choose a nominated partner.** They file the partnership return and keep the records.
2. **Register the partnership** online (with its own Government Gateway account) or on form SA400. HMRC issues a partnership Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR).
3. **Register every partner** for Self Assessment as a partner, online or on form SA401. Each partner gets their own UTR.
4. **Meet the deadline:** register by 5 October after the end of the tax year in which you started trading.

**Example:** you start trading on 1 September 2026 (tax year 2026/27). Register the partnership and both partners by 5 October 2027. The first returns are due online by 31 January 2028.

A partner who already files Self Assessment, for example as a landlord, still needs to tell HMRC they've joined a partnership.

## Business name, bank account and records

- **Business name:** you can trade under a business name (such as "Smith & Jones Cleaning"), but show all partners' names and an address on invoices and letters. Avoid "Ltd", "LLP" or sensitive words.
- **Business bank account:** keeping partnership money separate makes the records and tax return easier, and some banks require it.
- **Other registrations:** VAT if turnover passes £90,000, PAYE if you employ staff and CIS if you work in construction.

Keep these records from day one, for at least 5 years after the 31 January filing deadline:

- All sales and income
- All business expenses, with receipts
- Capital each partner puts in and drawings each takes out
- Assets bought, such as vans and equipment
- Bank statements
- The partnership agreement and profit shares

## How partners are taxed

1. **Partnership profit** is worked out once for the whole business: income minus allowable expenses.
2. **The profit is split by agreement:** partner salaries and interest on capital first, then the profit-sharing ratio.
3. **Each partner reports their share** on their own return and pays Income Tax and Class 4 NIC, alongside their other income.

Three things to know:

- **You're taxed whether you take the money or not.** Tax is due on your share of the profit, even if you leave it in the business.
- **Your share is added to your other income.** It sits on top of any salary, pension, rent or savings income, which can push you into a higher tax band.
- **There's no trading allowance.** The £1,000 trading allowance can't be used against partnership income, so partnership profit is always reported in full.

### Tax and National Insurance on your profit share (2026/27)

| Taxable income | Income Tax (England, Wales and NI) | Class 4 NIC |
|---|---|---|
| Up to £12,570 (personal allowance) | 0% | 0% |
| £12,571 to £50,270 | 20% | 6% |
| £50,271 to £125,140 | 40% | 2% |
| Over £125,140 | 45% | 2% |

- Scotland has different Income Tax bands.
- The personal allowance falls by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000.
- Class 2 NIC is no longer charged. If your profits are low, you can pay it voluntarily to protect your State Pension record.
- Where a company is a partner, its share is taxed under Corporation Tax instead. Special rules apply to these mixed partnerships.

### Worked example: three partners

The partnership makes a profit of £90,000 in 2026/27, shared 50% / 30% / 20%. None of the partners has other income, and the rates are for England.

| | Partner A (50%) | Partner B (30%) | Partner C (20%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share of profit | £45,000 | £27,000 | £18,000 |
| Taxable after £12,570 allowance | £32,430 | £14,430 | £5,430 |
| Income Tax at 20% | £6,486.00 | £2,886.00 | £1,086.00 |
| Class 4 NIC at 6% | £1,945.80 | £865.80 | £325.80 |
| **Total tax and NIC** | **£8,431.80** | **£3,751.80** | **£1,411.80** |
| Effective rate on share | 18.7% | 13.9% | 7.8% |

Together the partners pay £13,595.40 on £90,000 of profit, an effective rate of 15.1%. Each partner also makes payments on account for the next year if their bill is over £1,000, so Partner A's first January bill could be about 1.5 times the year's tax.

### Expenses, partner "salaries" and drawings

| Allowable expenses | Not expenses |
|---|---|
| Stock, materials and supplies | Partners' "salaries" |
| Staff wages and employer NIC | Partners' drawings |
| Rent, utilities and insurance | Interest paid on partners' capital |
| Vehicle and travel costs | Partners' own tax and NIC |
| Accountancy and bank charges | Private or personal spending |

**Partners' own expenses:** costs a partner pays personally for the business, such as their own car or phone, are claimed on the partnership return against that partner's share. Keep the receipts.

A partner "salary" is just a way of sharing profit. It's taxed as part of that partner's share, not as employment income, and there's no PAYE on it. Drawings are simply money taken out and don't change how much tax you pay.

### Accounting dates and the tax-year basis

Since 2024/25, partners are taxed on the profits of the tax year itself (6 April to 5 April), whatever the accounting year-end.

- **A 31 March or 5 April year-end is simplest.** Your accounts match the tax year, with no apportioning between years. We recommend it for new partnerships.
- **Other year-ends (such as 30 June)** mean profits are split across two accounting periods. You may need to estimate and correct later, which means more work and more accountancy cost.

**Transition profits:** partnerships that changed year-end under the 2023/24 reform are spreading the extra profit over five years, ending in 2027/28. Check that each partner's return includes their slice.

## Which tax returns you file

| Return | Filed by | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| Partnership return (SA800) | The nominated partner | Partnership income, expenses and profit, plus a partnership statement showing each partner's share |
| Each partner's return (SA100) | Each partner | Partnership pages showing their share, all their other income, their own tax bill and payments on account |

The partnership return can't be filed through HMRC's free online service, so use software or paper.

Partners use the **short partnership pages (SA104S)** for trading income only in a simple partnership, or the **full pages (SA104F)** if there is also savings, foreign income or other complex items.

## Key dates for 2025/26 and 2026/27

| Date | What's due |
|---|---|
| 5 October 2026 | Register a partnership that started in 2025/26 |
| 31 October 2026 | Paper partnership and partner returns for 2025/26 |
| 31 January 2027 | Online returns for 2025/26; pay the tax and the first payment on account |
| 31 July 2027 | Second payment on account for 2026/27 |
| 5 October 2027 | Register if you started in 2026/27 |
| 31 January 2028 | Online returns for 2026/27 |

Partnership and partner returns have the same deadlines. A late partnership return means a penalty for every partner, so the nominated partner should file early.

## Paying your tax: payments on account

Each partner pays their own tax. If a partner's bill is over £1,000 (and less than 80% is taxed at source), HMRC asks for two advance payments towards the next year.

**Example: Partner B in their first year**

| Payment | Amount | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Tax and Class 4 NIC for 2026/27 | £3,751.80 | 31 January 2028 |
| First payment on account for 2027/28 (50%) | £1,875.90 | 31 January 2028 |
| **January payment** | **£5,627.70** | **31 January 2028** |
| Second payment on account for 2027/28 | £1,875.90 | 31 July 2028 |

Each partner should set aside 20% to 25% of their share every month in a separate savings account. If profits are falling, payments on account can be reduced, but HMRC charges interest if you reduce them too far.

## Late return penalties apply to every partner

| How late | Penalty for each partner | With two partners |
|---|---|---|
| 1 day | £100 | £200 |
| 3 months | £10 a day for up to 90 days (up to £900) | Up to £1,800 |
| 6 months | £300 | £600 |
| 12 months | £300 | £600 |
| **Total for a year late** | **Up to £1,600 each** | **Up to £3,200** |

These penalties are on top of each partner's own late return penalties, late payment penalties and interest, and penalties for inaccurate returns. You can appeal if you had a reasonable excuse.

## Other taxes

### VAT for partnerships

- **Threshold:** register when taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, not just your accounting year, so check turnover monthly.
- **One registration** covers the partnership, not each partner. Register online or on form VAT1, with form VAT2 listing all partners, and tell HMRC when partners join or leave.
- **Making Tax Digital:** all VAT returns are filed through compatible software.
- **Voluntary registration** may make sense if your customers are VAT-registered.
- **Schemes that may help:** the Flat Rate Scheme (simpler, for small businesses), Cash Accounting (pay VAT when you're paid) and Annual Accounting (one return a year).

### Employing staff and the Construction Industry Scheme

**Employing staff:** the partnership registers as an employer with HMRC, runs payroll, sends RTI submissions and pays PAYE monthly. Workplace pension duties apply. Partners are never employees of their own partnership, so there's no PAYE on their profit shares. Our [payroll essentials guide](/insights/payroll-essentials-for-small-businesses/) covers employing staff in detail.

**Construction (CIS):**

- Partnership subcontractors may have 20% deducted from payments.
- Deductions are set against each partner's tax, in their profit shares.
- Contractors must verify subcontractors and file monthly CIS returns.
- Gross payment status is possible if the tests are met.

### Making Tax Digital and partnerships

Partnership income doesn't yet count towards Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, and partnerships have no start date. But a partner's own sole-trader or rental income does count, once it is over £50,000 now, £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. Those partners need MTD software for that income.

Get ready anyway: keep partnership records in software now. MTD for VAT already applies to VAT-registered partnerships. Read our [complete guide to Making Tax Digital](/insights/making-tax-digital-guide/) for how it works.

### Capital gains: selling assets or the business

When the partnership sells an asset, such as property or goodwill, each partner is treated as owning their share and pays Capital Gains Tax on their part of the gain.

| Relief | What it does | Rate or limit |
|---|---|---|
| Business Asset Disposal Relief | A lower rate on gains when selling all or part of a business | 18% from 6 April 2026 (lifetime limit £1 million) |
| Rollover relief | Defers the gain if proceeds are reinvested in new business assets | Reinvest within 1 year before to 3 years after |
| Incorporation relief | Defers the gain when the business moves into a company for shares | Automatic if the conditions are met |
| Annual exempt amount | Each partner's tax-free gains | £3,000 |

Changing profit shares can also be a disposal of part of each partner's interest in the assets. Talk to us before reorganising.

## Partners joining and leaving

**When a partner joins:**

- The new partner registers with HMRC as a partner.
- Update the agreement and profit shares from the joining date.
- Tell HMRC VAT if the partnership is registered.
- Their share of profit starts from the date they join.

**When a partner leaves:**

- Tell HMRC the leaving date.
- They're still taxed on their share up to that date.
- Settle their capital account.
- Check for any capital gains on their share of the assets.

Without a written agreement, a partner leaving or dying can legally end a general partnership. Check that your agreement lets the business continue.

## LLPs and limited partnerships

**LLP basics:** an LLP is registered at Companies House, its members' liability is limited, it needs at least 2 designated members and it is taxed like a partnership.

**LLP extra duties:**

- Annual accounts filed at Companies House
- A confirmation statement every year
- Identity verification for members
- Possibly an audit if the LLP is large

**Salaried members:** LLP members paid a mostly fixed amount may be taxed as employees. Three conditions decide this, so get advice when setting up roles.

**Limited partnerships (LPs)** have at least one general partner with unlimited liability, and limited partners who can't manage the business. They're mainly used for investment funds.

## Ending the partnership or becoming a company

**Closing down:**

- File a final partnership return to the closing date.
- Each partner reports their final share.
- Deregister for VAT and PAYE.
- Deal with the assets: sales may trigger Capital Gains Tax.

**Moving into a limited company:**

- This can make sense as profits grow.
- Incorporation relief can defer Capital Gains Tax.
- Contracts, the bank account, VAT and staff move to the company.
- Plan the timing around the tax year.

As a rule of thumb, once each partner's share regularly exceeds the higher-rate threshold and you don't need all the profit, a company may save tax. We'll run the numbers for you.

## Your partnership checklist

1. Sign a written partnership agreement.
2. Choose a nominated partner.
3. Register the partnership and every partner with HMRC by 5 October.
4. Open a business bank account.
5. Set up bookkeeping software and keep receipts.
6. Agree a 31 March or 5 April year-end.
7. Each partner saves 20% to 25% of their share for tax.
8. Watch turnover against the £90,000 VAT threshold.
9. File the partnership return and every partner's return by 31 January.
10. Review the structure each year as profits grow.
