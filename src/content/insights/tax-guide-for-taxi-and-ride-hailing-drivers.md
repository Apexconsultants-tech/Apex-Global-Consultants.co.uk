Whether you drive for Uber, Bolt, FreeNow or a local minicab firm, you are usually self-employed, which means you are responsible for reporting your income and paying your own tax. This guide covers the 2026/27 tax year.

## Do I need to file a tax return?

- **Yes, if your driving income is over £1,000 a year** (6 April to 5 April), even if you also have a PAYE job.
- **New to driving?** Register with HMRC by 5 October after the end of your first tax year to get a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR).
- **Trading allowance:** the first £1,000 is tax-free. This is due to rise to £3,000, but the date has not yet been confirmed.
- **Driving for several apps?** Add all your driving income together.

## What income to declare

Declare your **full fare**, not just what reaches your bank account, and then claim the app fee as an expense.

| Example | £ |
|---|---|
| Fare paid by the passenger | 20 |
| App fee | (5) |
| Paid to you | 15 |

In this example you declare the full £20 and claim the £5 app fee as an expense. HMRC receives the gross figure from the app, so your return needs to match it.

- Include tips, bonuses, cancellation fees and cash fares.
- Use each app's annual tax summary to get your figures.

## Expenses: two ways to claim your car costs

Pick one method per car and keep using it for that car.

### A. Mileage rate

- **55p per business mile** for the first 10,000 miles in 2026/27 (45p in 2025/26), then a lower rate.
- Covers fuel, insurance, repairs, road tax, finance and wear and tear.
- You only need a mileage log.

### B. Actual costs

- Your real costs multiplied by your business-use percentage.
- Includes fuel, hire-and-reward insurance, servicing, tyres, MOT, road tax, rental or lease payments, finance interest and capital allowances.
- Keep every receipt.

**Which suits you?** If you rent or lease your car, or have high running costs, actual costs usually work better. If you drive a cheap-to-run car (a hybrid or high-mpg model) and want simple records, the mileage rate is often the better choice.

### Claim these on top, whichever method you use

- App commission and service fees
- Driver and vehicle licences, DBS checks and medicals
- Phone and data (the business share)
- Parking, tolls, Congestion Charge and Clean Air Zone charges
- Cleaning and valeting, and accountancy fees

You can't claim fines, penalties or private journeys.

## Tax is on profit, not fares

| Profit (2026/27) | Income tax | Class 4 NI |
|---|---|---|
| Up to £12,570 | 0% | 0% |
| £12,571 – £50,270 | 20% | 6% |
| £50,271 – £125,140 | 40% | 2% |
| Over £125,140 | 45% | 2% |

These are the rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scotland's income tax bands differ. Class 2 National Insurance is now voluntary.

### Example: a full-time Uber driver with no other income

| | £ |
|---|---|
| Gross fares and tips | 40,000 |
| Less app fees | (10,000) |
| Less car rental, fuel, phone and licences | (12,700) |
| **Profit** | **17,300** |
| Income tax: (17,300 − 12,570) × 20% | 946 |
| Class 4 NI: (17,300 − 12,570) × 6% | 284 |
| **Total tax** | **1,230** |

## Key dates

| Date | What's due |
|---|---|
| 31 January 2027 | File your 2025/26 return online and pay; first payment on account for 2026/27 |
| 31 July 2027 | Second payment on account for 2026/27 |
| 5 October 2027 | Register with HMRC if you started driving in 2026/27 |

## Pay on time

- If your tax bill is over £1,000, you usually pay half of next year's tax in advance, in January and July (payments on account).
- Set aside 15–20% of your profit each month.
- A late return means a £100 penalty, and more the longer it goes on.

## Making Tax Digital

Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, you'll need to keep digital records and send quarterly updates to HMRC if your gross fares (together with any other self-employment or property income) exceed:

| From | Gross income over |
|---|---|
| April 2026 | £50,000 |
| April 2027 | £30,000 |
| April 2028 | £20,000 |

## Received a letter from HMRC?

Ride-hailing apps now report drivers' earnings to HMRC, so it can compare them with what you've declared. Don't ignore a letter: telling HMRC first about anything you've missed means lower penalties. We can help you make the disclosure.

## Keep your records for at least five years

- App tax summaries, cash records and mileage logs
- All car, licence, phone and parking receipts
