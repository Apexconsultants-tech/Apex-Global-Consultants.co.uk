Companies House can remove a limited company from the register if it believes the company is no longer in business. The most common reason is simple: overdue accounts or a missing confirmation statement. This guide explains the deadlines, the penalties, how strike-off works, how to stop it and what to do if your company has already been dissolved.

## Had a letter from Companies House? Start here

| Your situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| The company is already shown as **dissolved** | Stop using the company bank account and [look at restoration](#restoring-a-dissolved-company) |
| A **proposal to strike off** (First Gazette notice) has been published | Act now: follow the [urgent action plan](#your-urgent-action-plan) |
| Accounts or the confirmation statement are **overdue**, but no notice yet | [Catch up on your filings](#companies-house-late-filing-penalties) before strike-off starts |

## The three filings every limited company makes

Even a dormant company that has never traded must file with Companies House. Missing these filings is the main reason companies are struck off.

| Filing | Filed with | When it's due |
|---|---|---|
| Annual accounts | Companies House | 9 months after the company's year-end (private companies) |
| Confirmation statement | Companies House | At least once every 12 months, within 14 days of the end of the review period |
| Company Tax Return (CT600) | HMRC | 12 months after the end of the accounting period |

### When your accounts are due

| Situation | Deadline | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Normal year (private company) | 9 months after the year-end, to the exact day | Year-end 4 April: due 4 January, not 31 January |
| First accounts covering more than 12 months | 21 months after incorporation, or 3 months after the year-end if later | Incorporated 1 January 2025, year-end 31 January 2026: due 1 October 2026 |
| First accounts covering 12 months or less | The normal 9-month deadline | |
| Dormant company | The same deadlines apply | Dormant accounts must still be filed |

**Need more time?** You can only apply for an extension before the deadline, and only if an unplanned event stops you filing. Apply as early as possible.

Put the exact due date in your diary: deadlines don't move to the end of the month.

### The confirmation statement

The confirmation statement is a yearly check that Companies House holds the right information about your company. It doesn't include any financial figures.

- **Fee:** £50 online or £110 on paper (from 1 February 2026).
- **Deadline:** within 14 days of the end of your review period, at least once every 12 months, including for dormant companies.
- **Not filing:** a fine of up to £5,000, and the company may be struck off.

What you check and update:

- Registered office address
- Directors and company secretary
- Shareholders and share capital
- People with significant control (PSCs)
- Business activity (SIC codes)

All directors must have verified their identity with Companies House before the statement can be filed. See [director identity verification](#director-identity-verification) below.

### Corporation Tax: the HMRC side

- **The CT600 return** is due 12 months after the end of the accounting period and is filed online with your accounts and tax computations. It must be filed if HMRC has sent a notice to file, even if no tax is due.
- **Corporation Tax** is usually payable 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period, so the tax is normally paid before the return is due. Interest is charged on late payment.

Companies House and HMRC are separate. Filing accounts at Companies House does not file your tax return, and filing with HMRC does not file your Companies House accounts. If your Companies House accounts are overdue, your CT600 is often overdue too, so check both.

## Companies House late filing penalties

| Accounts filed late by | Penalty (private company) | If also late last year |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 1 month | £150 | £300 |
| 1 to 3 months | £375 | £750 |
| 3 to 6 months | £750 | £1,500 |
| More than 6 months | £1,500 | £3,000 |

- Penalties are issued automatically.
- They double if the accounts were late two years in a row.
- Persistent non-filing can lead to fines and the company being struck off.

The confirmation statement has no late filing penalty, but not filing it is a criminal offence and a common trigger for strike-off. File the moment the accounts are ready: the penalty band is based on when they arrive.

### HMRC penalties for a late Company Tax Return

| How late | Penalty |
|---|---|
| 1 day | £200 |
| 3 months | Another £200 |
| 6 months | HMRC estimates your tax (a "tax determination") and charges 10% of the unpaid tax |
| 12 months | Another 10% of any unpaid tax |

If the return is late three times in a row, each £200 penalty becomes £1,000. You can't appeal a tax determination: it is replaced when you file the actual return and pay the real tax.

### Appealing a Companies House penalty

An appeal needs a specific reason, with dates and details, showing circumstances genuinely beyond your control, such as sudden serious illness. Companies House doesn't collect the penalty while it considers the appeal.

Reasons that are unlikely to succeed:

- It was the company's first accounts, or the company is dormant
- You can't afford the penalty
- Your accountant or someone else made a mistake
- You didn't know how to file, or there were post delays
- The directors were overseas

If your appeal is rejected:

1. Write to the senior casework manager with any new information.
2. Ask the independent adjudicators to review it.
3. Make a final application to the Registrar of Companies.

## How compulsory strike-off works

If Companies House believes your company is no longer in business, usually because filings are missing, it can remove it from the register.

1. **Filings are missed:** the accounts or confirmation statement are overdue.
2. **Companies House contacts you:** letters ask the company to file or explain.
3. **First Gazette notice:** a public notice that the company will be struck off.
4. **At least 2 months:** your window to file and stop it.
5. **Struck off and dissolved:** a second Gazette notice is published and the company no longer exists.

The Gazette is public. Banks, suppliers and credit agencies watch it, so a strike-off notice can affect your credit and contracts even before the company is removed.

### Why the registrar can strike a company off

| Reason | What happens | Time after the Gazette notice |
|---|---|---|
| The company appears not to be in business (for example, annual documents missing or no directors) | The registrar contacts the company, then publishes a notice | Not less than 2 months |
| The registered office was moved to Companies House's default address | 28 days to give a new address, otherwise a notice is published | Not less than 2 months |
| The company was registered on a false basis | A notice of intent is published | Not less than 28 days |

The fix depends on the reason: reply to Companies House's letters promptly, file any outstanding documents, or give a valid registered office address.

## How to stop a strike-off

**Directors: file what's missing**

- Reply to any Companies House letter straight away.
- File the overdue confirmation statement and accounts.
- Keep proof of each filing and check the company's filing history.

**Shareholders and creditors: object to the strike-off**

- Shareholders and interested parties, such as creditors owed money, can object.
- An objection can only be made after the first Gazette notice, with evidence (for example, invoices).
- It must reach Companies House at least 2 weeks before the date in the notice.
- From 1 December 2026, objections must be made online through Companies House's "Make an objection" service. Email objections are no longer accepted.

Once the company is dissolved it's too late to object: it has to be restored instead.

### Your urgent action plan

1. **Check the company record.** Find the Gazette notice date and see which filings are overdue.
2. **Verify the directors' identity.** This is needed before the confirmation statement can be filed.
3. **File the confirmation statement** online (£50), checking every detail is up to date.
4. **Prepare and file the accounts**, or dormant accounts if the company didn't trade.
5. **Sort out the CT600 with HMRC.** File the return and pay any Corporation Tax due.
6. **Confirm and prevent.** Check the filing history, keep proof and set reminders for next year.

## What dissolution means

- **The company no longer exists.** It can't legally trade, sign contracts or hold property until it's restored.
- **The bank account is frozen** from the date of dissolution. It can't receive payments, and money in it passes to the Crown.
- **Assets go to the Crown.** Property and assets become "bona vacantia" (ownerless goods) and belong to the Crown.

Dissolution doesn't wipe out your problems: unpaid tax, debts and penalties still have to be dealt with, and restoring the company means bringing every filing up to date.

## Restoring a dissolved company

| | Administrative restoration | Restoration by court order |
|---|---|---|
| Time limit | Up to 6 years after dissolution | Generally within 6 years (no limit for personal injury claims) |
| Who can apply | A former director or member (shareholder) at the time of strike-off | A wider group, including former directors, members and creditors |
| Main conditions | The company was in business or in operation when struck off; not available after voluntary strike-off | Decided by the court |
| Before applying | File all overdue documents, pay outstanding penalties and get a Crown bona vacantia waiver if needed | The court may require you to pay the Crown's and registrar's costs |
| How | Form RT01 plus a fee (£341 by post from 1 February 2026) | A court application, usually with a solicitor |

Once restored, the company is treated as if it had never been struck off. If administrative restoration is refused, you can apply to the court within 28 days.

If your company is dissolved, don't use the old company bank account or trade in the company's name while you sort out restoration.

## Closing a company you no longer need

Rather than let a company be struck off for missing filings, directors can apply to close it properly through voluntary strike-off (form DS01). The fee is £13 online or £18 on paper (from 1 February 2026), and the company is dissolved at least 2 months after the Gazette notice.

You can apply if:

- The majority of directors sign (both, if there are two).
- In the last 3 months the company hasn't traded, changed its name or sold stock.
- It isn't in insolvency proceedings.
- HMRC matters, such as the final CT600 and any refunds, are settled first.

You must send a copy of the application to shareholders, creditors, employees, pension trustees and any directors who didn't sign within 7 days, and keep doing so for anyone new until the company is dissolved. Failing to do so is an offence.

Voluntary strike-off is not for a company that can't pay its debts. That needs insolvency advice instead.

## Director identity verification

Since 18 November 2025, Companies House has required directors and people with significant control (PSCs) to verify their identity.

| Who | When |
|---|---|
| New directors | Before they can be appointed or form a company |
| Existing directors | Confirmed with the company's next confirmation statement (12-month transition) |
| Existing PSCs | Within the 12-month transition period |

1. **Verify:** free online through GOV.UK One Login, or through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP).
2. **Get your personal code:** Companies House gives you a personal code once you're verified.
3. **Link it to your roles:** use the code and a verification statement for each company role you hold.

## Stay-compliant checklist

- Know your year-end and your exact accounts deadline.
- Diary your confirmation statement date.
- Make sure every director has verified their identity.
- Keep the registered office and email address up to date.
- Open and reply to all Companies House and HMRC post.
- File the accounts and CT600 together, well before the deadline.
- Pay Corporation Tax 9 months and 1 day after the year-end.
- Dormant? File dormant accounts anyway, or close the company properly.
