Making Tax Digital (MTD) is HMRC's programme to move tax records and returns online. Instead of one paper or online return a year, businesses keep digital records and send HMRC regular updates through compatible software.

HMRC's aim is to reduce avoidable mistakes, which it says make up a large part of the tax gap. For you, regular updates give a clearer picture of profits and tax during the year, and records flow straight from software to HMRC with fewer manual steps.

> **What doesn't change:** how much tax you pay, the tax rates, and the 31 January and 31 July payment dates. MTD changes how and how often you report, not what you owe.

## Who MTD covers today

- **MTD for VAT:** all VAT-registered businesses, since April 2022.
- **MTD for Income Tax:** sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000, from 6 April 2026.
- **Corporation Tax:** there are no current plans to introduce MTD.

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| April 2019 | MTD for VAT starts for businesses above the VAT threshold |
| April 2022 | MTD for VAT extends to all VAT-registered businesses, whatever their turnover |
| April 2026 | MTD for Income Tax: sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 |
| April 2027 | Income Tax threshold falls to £30,000 |
| April 2028 | Income Tax threshold falls to £20,000 |
| April 2029 | E-invoicing (planned): VAT invoices in a set electronic format |

Partnerships are not yet in MTD for Income Tax and have no start date. The e-invoicing date has been announced, but the detailed rules are still to come.

## Who must use MTD for Income Tax, and when

| Qualifying income | MTD starts | Based on income in |
|---|---|---|
| Over £50,000 | 6 April 2026 | 2024/25 tax return |
| Over £30,000 | 6 April 2027 | 2025/26 tax return |
| Over £20,000 | 6 April 2028 | 2026/27 tax return |
| £20,000 or less | Not required (you can join voluntarily) | – |

### What counts as qualifying income?

Qualifying income is your **gross income (turnover, before expenses)** from:

- **Self-employment:** all your sole-trader businesses added together.
- **Property:** UK and overseas rental income; your share if a property is jointly owned.

It does not include employment (PAYE) and pension income, dividends and savings interest, or your share of partnership profits.

**Example.** Priya earns a £45,000 salary, £28,000 gross from consulting and £26,000 rent from a flat she owns with her partner (her share is £13,000). Her qualifying income is £28,000 + £13,000 = £41,000; her salary is ignored. She joins MTD from April 2027 if her 2025/26 figures are the same.

Remember it's turnover, not profit. A landlord with £35,000 of rent and a £25,000 mortgage interest bill is still over £30,000.

### Which year decides when you start?

HMRC looks at the tax return from two years earlier:

- your **2024/25** return (filed by 31 January 2026) decides whether you start on **6 April 2026**
- your **2025/26** return (filed by 31 January 2027) decides whether you start on **6 April 2027**
- your **2026/27** return (filed by 31 January 2028) decides whether you start on **6 April 2028**

HMRC writes to people it expects to be in scope, but you must still check and sign up yourself, even without a letter. Once you are in, you stay in: you can leave only if your qualifying income stays under the threshold for three tax years in a row, or you stop the business. If you have no National Insurance number on 5 April before a tax year, you are exempt for that year.

## Exemptions and deferrals

**Automatically exempt:**

- people with no National Insurance number on 5 April before the tax year
- ministers of religion
- Lloyd's underwriters
- people claiming Blind Person's Allowance or Married Couple's Allowance
- personal representatives (executors)
- people under a power of attorney or deputyship who can't provide the information

**Deferred, so not in MTD for 2026/27:**

- trustees (SA107 pages in the 2024/25 return)
- foster carers and shared lives carers
- people who made averaging claims (farmers, creative artists)
- non-residents filing SA109 pages, from April 2027 at the earliest

**You can apply to be treated as digitally excluded** if it is not reasonably practicable for you to use software, for example because of age, disability or location, or if your religious beliefs are incompatible with electronic communications. Age alone isn't enough: you must explain why. Apply to HMRC by phone or letter; HMRC aims to reply within 28 days.

Partnership income doesn't count yet. A partner who also has sole-trader or property income may still be in scope for that income.

## How a year in MTD works

1. **Keep digital records.** Record each sale and expense in compatible software.
2. **Send four quarterly updates.** Each one gives HMRC your cumulative income and expense totals.
3. **See tax estimates.** HMRC shows an estimate of your tax after each update.
4. **Make year-end adjustments.** Add accounting and tax adjustments, reliefs and allowances.
5. **Submit your final declaration** by 31 January. It replaces the tax return.

Quarterly updates are simple summaries. Other income, such as salary or dividends, goes in at the year end.

### Quarterly update periods and deadlines

| Update | Standard period (cumulative) | Calendar-quarter election | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarter 1 | 6 April – 5 July | 1 April – 30 June | 7 August |
| Quarter 2 | 6 April – 5 October | 1 April – 30 September | 7 November |
| Quarter 3 | 6 April – 5 January | 1 April – 31 December | 7 February |
| Quarter 4 | 6 April – 5 April | 1 April – 31 March | 7 May |
| Final declaration | Whole tax year | Whole tax year | 31 January |

Because each update covers the year so far, later updates can fix earlier mistakes. You can also choose calendar quarters ending on the last day of the month to match your bookkeeping.

For 2026/27, the first-year updates are due on 7 August 2026, 7 November 2026, 7 February 2027 and 7 May 2027. The first final declaration is due by 31 January 2028.

### Digital records and software rules

- **Record every transaction** with its date, amount and category (for example rent, fuel or repairs), in software or in a spreadsheet linked to software.
- **Use digital links.** Data must pass between programs digitally, with no retyping or copy-and-paste between your records and the submission. Linked files, imports, APIs and bank feeds all count.
- **Spreadsheets are still allowed,** but you need bridging software to send updates to HMRC. This suits simple businesses and landlords.
- **Receipts and invoices:** you don't have to scan every receipt. Keep the originals or images for at least five years after the 31 January deadline, as now.
- **Small cash items:** cash takings and small purchases can be totalled daily and recorded as one entry, if your software allows.

### Paying your tax

| Payment | Due date |
|---|---|
| Balancing payment for the year | 31 January |
| 1st payment on account | 31 January |
| 2nd payment on account | 31 July |

Payment dates and payments on account don't change under MTD.

## Penalties under MTD for Income Tax

**Late submission works on points.** You get one point for each late quarterly update or final declaration. Four points within two years means a £200 penalty, and £200 for each later late submission. Points expire after two years if you stay on time.

**2026/27 soft landing:** no points for late quarterly updates in the first year (announced at the November 2025 Budget). The updates are still compulsory, and full penalties apply from 2027/28.

**Late payment penalties are percentages:**

| When tax is still unpaid | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Up to day 15 | None (30-day grace in the first year) |
| Day 16 (or 31) | 3% of tax then unpaid |
| Day 31 | Another 3% of tax still unpaid |
| From day 31 | 10% a year on the unpaid tax |
| Interest | Base rate + 4%, from the due date |

LITRG reports that the 3% rates rise to 4% from 2027/28. Penalties don't apply to payments on account, and agreeing a Time to Pay arrangement early stops them. If you have a reasonable excuse, points and penalties can be appealed.

## MTD for VAT: the rules today

All VAT-registered businesses must use MTD for VAT. The VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover in a rolling 12 months. You must:

- keep VAT records digitally
- submit VAT returns through compatible software
- keep digital links between your records and the return

Late returns earn points, and you pay a £200 penalty once you reach the points threshold for your return frequency:

| Return frequency | Points for £200 penalty |
|---|---|
| Annual | 2 |
| Quarterly | 4 |
| Monthly | 5 |

Late VAT payments attract 3% after 15 days, another 3% after 30 days, then 10% a year until paid, plus late payment interest. VAT and MTD for Income Tax share the same points-based late filing system, but points for each tax are counted separately, so businesses in both should keep both on time.

## What's next for MTD

- **E-invoicing from April 2029:** announced at Budget 2025, with VAT invoices in a set electronic format. Detailed rules are still to come.
- **Lower thresholds:** the Income Tax threshold falls to £30,000 (2027) and £20,000 (2028). Nothing has been decided below £20,000.
- **Partnerships:** not yet in MTD for Income Tax, with no start date set.
- **Corporation Tax:** no current plans for MTD.

Our view: digital record-keeping is now the long-term direction for every UK tax. Moving your books to software now, even if you're not yet in scope, makes each future phase easier.

## Choosing MTD software

**Must have:**

- on HMRC's list of software compatible with MTD for Income Tax (or VAT)
- covers your income types: self-employment, UK and foreign property
- lets your accountant connect as your agent
- includes the final declaration, not just quarterly updates

**Nice to have:** bank feeds that import transactions automatically, receipt capture from your phone, and tax estimates and reminders before each deadline.

There are three routes: an **all-in-one** product for bookkeeping and filing, **bridging** software that keeps your spreadsheet and sends updates through a link, or **free options** that some products offer for simple needs. Check the product supports everything you need before you commit.

## Your MTD readiness checklist

1. Work out your qualifying income from your latest tax return.
2. Confirm your start date.
3. Check whether an exemption or deferral applies.
4. Choose compatible software, or bridging software for spreadsheets.
5. Open a separate business bank account if you don't have one.
6. Move your records into software and connect bank feeds.
7. Authorise your accountant as your agent with HMRC.
8. Sign up for MTD through HMRC before your first quarter ends.
9. Put the quarterly deadlines in your diary: 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.
10. Set aside tax each month for 31 January and 31 July.

## Common questions

**Do I file quarterly even with no income in a quarter?** Yes. Each update is still due; it can show nil.

**Does MTD change how much tax I pay?** No. It changes how you report, not the tax or the payment dates.

**My accounting year ends on 31 December.** Updates still follow the tax year (or calendar quarters), with adjustments at the year end.

**Can I join early?** Yes. You can sign up voluntarily before you're required to.

**Can I keep using spreadsheets?** Yes, with bridging software to send the figures to HMRC.

**I own a property jointly. What counts?** Only your share of the gross rent counts towards the threshold.

**What if my income drops below the threshold?** You can leave after three tax years in a row below it.

**Can my accountant do it for me?** Yes. Once authorised as your agent, we can keep your records and submit your updates.
