The International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) has issued the third edition of the IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard, following its second comprehensive review. It moves the standard closer to full IFRS while keeping it built for smaller entities.

## The big picture

- **Third edition,** after the IASB's second comprehensive review.
- **Six sections revised,** but no change to leases yet.
- **Effective 1 January 2027** for periods starting on or after that date. Early adoption is allowed.

**Who it's for:** entities without public accountability that publish general-purpose financial statements.

## What changes, and what it now follows

| Section | Topic | Now aligned with |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Concepts and pervasive principles | 2018 Conceptual Framework |
| 9 | Consolidated and separate financial statements | Single control model |
| 11 | Financial instruments | Two sections merged; simpler classification |
| 12 | Fair value measurement (new) | IFRS 13 |
| 19 | Business combinations | IFRS 3 |
| 23 | Revenue from contracts with customers | IFRS 15 five-step model |
| 20 | Leases | No change: IFRS 16 alignment deferred |

## Section by section: what it means for you

| Section | What changed | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| 2 Concepts | Updated to the 2018 Conceptual Framework. | Clearer definitions; check your accounting policies still fit. |
| 9 Consolidation | One model for deciding control. | Reassess which entities you control and must consolidate. |
| 11 Financial instruments | Two sections merged; simpler classification; financial guarantee rules. | Reclassify instruments, account for guarantees and expand disclosures. |
| 12 Fair value | New section based on IFRS 13. | Use one consistent fair value method, with more disclosure. |
| 19 Business combinations | Reflects IFRS 3, with a new definition of a business. | Check whether an acquisition counts as a business. |

## Revenue now follows IFRS 15's five steps, simplified

Section 23 now uses a simplified version of the IFRS 15 five-step model:

1. Identify the contract.
2. Find the performance obligations.
3. Set the transaction price.
4. Allocate the price to the obligations.
5. Recognise revenue as each obligation is satisfied.

If you sell bundled deliverables, offer discounts or charge variable fees, the timing of your revenue may change.

## Your checklist

- Map your revenue contracts against the five steps.
- Review your financial instruments and any guarantees you have given.
- Reassess control across your group.
- Decide whether early adoption makes sense for you.
