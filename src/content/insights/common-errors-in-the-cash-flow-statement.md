The cash flow statement shows a company's liquidity and its ability to generate cash, and the Financial Reporting Council (FRC) regularly challenges it. The FRC's reviews keep finding the same three kinds of problem: **classification**, **non-cash items** and **netting**.

Here are the nine errors that come up most often, grouped by type, and how to get each one right.

## The errors at a glance

| Type | Errors |
|---|---|
| Classification (4) | Interest classification · Acquiring a subsidiary · Contingent consideration · Changes in ownership |
| Non-cash items (2) | Right-of-use assets · Non-cash transactions |
| Cash and netting (2) | Bank overdrafts · Intragroup loans |
| Consistency (1) | Inconsistent amounts |

## Wrong vs right: the fix for each error

### 1. Inconsistent amounts (consistency)

- **What goes wrong:** line items and amounts differ from those in the other statements and notes.
- **Getting it right:** reconcile every line to the balance sheet, profit and loss account and notes, and use the same descriptions.

### 2. Interest classification (classification)

- **What goes wrong:** lease interest is shown in operating activities, while loan interest is shown in financing.
- **Getting it right:** choose one policy for interest paid and apply it to all interest, leases included.

### 3. Bank overdrafts (cash and netting)

- **What goes wrong:** overdrafts that are repayable on demand and used in cash management are excluded from cash.
- **Getting it right:** include them in cash and cash equivalents, and reconcile to the balance sheet.

### 4. Intragroup loans (cash and netting)

- **What goes wrong:** intragroup borrowing and lending are offset into one figure.
- **Getting it right:** show gross flows, and net only where IAS 7 allows (for example, items with a quick turnover and short maturity).

### 5. Right-of-use assets (non-cash items)

- **What goes wrong:** additions to right-of-use assets are included as investing cash flows.
- **Getting it right:** leave them out, because no cash moved. Show lease payments when they are paid, and disclose the addition.

### 6. Acquiring a subsidiary (classification)

- **What goes wrong:** the cost of the investment in the separate accounts is shown as operating.
- **Getting it right:** in the separate accounts, it is investing. In the group accounts, acquisition costs are expensed, so they are operating.

### 7. Contingent consideration (classification)

- **What goes wrong:** amounts paid above the liability first recognised are shown as investing.
- **Getting it right:** payments up to the amount recognised at acquisition are investing; any excess is operating.

### 8. Changes in ownership (classification)

- **What goes wrong:** changes in stakes are misclassified in the group cash flow statement.
- **Getting it right:** if control is kept, it is an equity transaction, so financing. If control is lost, it is investing.

### 9. Non-cash transactions (non-cash items)

- **What goes wrong:** material non-cash transactions are missing from the notes.
- **Getting it right:** disclose them in the notes, as IAS 7 requires.

## Coming in 2027: IFRS 18

IFRS 18 also amends IAS 7 from 1 January 2027. Most companies will show interest paid in financing activities, and interest and dividends received in investing activities, removing today's choice. Error 2 then largely disappears.
